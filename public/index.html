Task Message Studio

What difference can a task message make to a worker's experience?

A Crowd Task Message Design System that considers the crowd worker's intrinsic motivation and task experience

Task Message Studio designs before- and after-task communication tailored to each task's characteristics, so workers can understand the meaning of the task and their own contribution.

Why do we work?

Some work we do because it is enjoyable or satisfying in itself.
Some work we do to obtain a reward or an outcome.

Intrinsic Motivation

Motivation from the work itself

Motivation to act because the activity itself is fun, interesting, or satisfying.

Extrinsic Motivation

Motivation from an external outcome

Motivation to act in order to obtain a reward, evaluation, recognition, or a specific outcome.

This can show up as follows in crowd work.

"The results of this task are used to improve the quality of AI data."

The worker can understand why the task is needed and the meaning of their contribution.

"You will receive a reward for completing this task."

The worker can take part in the task with the external outcome of a reward in mind.

So what about crowd work?

Monetary reward matters in crowd work. Task Message Studio is not a system that denies or tries to replace the importance of reward.

Instead, it supports task communication so that workers experience the task as more than just "something you do to get paid" — helping them understand their own judgment, the meaning of the task, and their contribution.

Three basic psychological needs that support a crowd worker's task experience

Self-Determination Theory (SDT) explains that the more Autonomy, Competence, and Relatedness are satisfied, the more favorable the conditions become for voluntary engagement and sustained intrinsic motivation.

Autonomy

"I can choose and judge for myself."

Supports workers in feeling that their judgment and choices are respected, rather than overly controlled.

Competence

"I can do this task well."

Recognizes the worker's judgment and ability to contribute, supporting a sense that they can perform effectively.

Relatedness

"My work is connected to someone."

Supports the feeling that one's contribution is connected to other people or to a larger purpose.

The message is the first point of contact between requester and worker.

Even for the same task, how the message is delivered can change the meaning and motivation a worker experiences. Task Message Studio first analyzes the task's characteristics, then selects or prioritizes the needed SDT elements before reflecting them in the worker message.

Crowdworker Survey (N=120)

In our preliminary study, we surveyed 120 crowdworkers. The Core and Supporting Frames are determined from workers' preferred motivational framing for each task type.

Task TypeCoreSupporting3rd

1st place is applied as Core and 2nd place as Supporting during generation. 3rd place is for reference only and is not used in message generation.

Message Length 4–5 sentences

Medium length was preferred by 66.7% (80/120) of surveyed crowdworkers. The system operationalizes this preference as 4–5 sentences for each Before-task and After-task message.

Messages written together by our system and the requester are delivered to the worker before they start the task and after they finish it.

Not every message fixes all three elements. The system analyzes the task's burden and responsibility, repetitiveness, and social meaning to prioritize the support that is actually needed.
